How the agent actually moves your money

One request, traced end to end — from a tap in the browser to a hash-chained row in SQLite. The AI only ever proposes; two independent, non-AI checks decide.

  • ALLOW — goes straight through
  • REQUIRE APPROVAL — waits for a tap
  • DENY — refused, still logged
  • external system
  • the two policy gates

Why two gates, not one

Gate 1 sits in front of the LLM-facing path, inside the orchestrator, and runs before any money tool is allowed to fire — regardless of what the model claims it already checked.

Gate 2 lives inside the state machine that actually creates the payment (money_action_service), and runs its own independent check_policy() call, freezing that decision onto the record.

A confused or adversarial model cannot talk its way past either one: the policy engine has no memory of persuasion, and a DENY at Gate 1 means Gate 2, Razorpay, and the ledger are never even reached.
